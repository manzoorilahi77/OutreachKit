01 · Summary

The verdict

Zero replies was not mainly a wording problem. Three things stacked up. The channels could not carry the message. The buyer was wrong for software projects. And a buyer who checked Famysys found large claims with no names, reviews or team behind them. Fix delivery and trust first, switch to US agencies and companies with open developer roles, and open with small paid tests instead of a pitch.

Cause 1 · Confirmed

The channels couldn't deliver it

Instagram shows a non-follower's first message as a text-only request, so the demo screenshot could not appear. Cold email went out from a free Gmail address, which Google's policy forbids for unsolicited commercial mail and which can never build sender reputation.

Cause 2 · Likely

The wrong buyer for software

Local trades with no website have small budgets and rarely buy software from a firm they don't know. Small, simple web work is also the work most exposed to DIY and AI tools.

Cause 3 · Confirmed

Nothing to verify

famysys.com claims “SOC2 Type II Compliant”, “48+ engagements”, “120+ systems in production” and “99.9% uptime”, but names no clients, shows no team and has no reviews. 74% of B2B buyers read reviews before buying.

Fix first, this week.
  1. Stop cold email from famysys.sales@gmail.com. Set up a separate sending domain on Google Workspace with SPF, DKIM and DMARC, and start warming it up.
  2. Remove or prove every number and the SOC 2 claim on famysys.com. Add a team page with the founder's real name.
  3. Open a free Clutch profile and ask five past clients for reviews.
  4. Stop Instagram DMs for software work. Move that time to LinkedIn with US agency owners.
  5. Update the us-it-lead-drafts company profile with the new sending address, real results with numbers and your US overlap hours.

03 · Phase 2

What the research says

E marks evidence (surveys, platform rules, law, measured data). P marks a practitioner or vendor view. Full links are in section 10. Several popular statistics could not be traced to a primary source and are left out.

How US buyers find a software firm

  • E 51% of B2B software buyers start research in an AI chatbot, up from 29% in April 2025; 33% bought from a vendor they hadn't known before (G2, 2026).
  • E 79% already knew the vendor before researching; the average shortlist is 2.7 vendors; vendor brochures ranked last (TrustRadius, July 2026).
  • E Fewer than 60% of professional-services buyers ask for referrals, down from over 70% in 2013 (Hinge, 2018).
  • No current survey splits discovery between referrals, Clutch, Upwork, Google and LinkedIn for software services. Not found.

Who buys, and for how much

  • E 55% of US small businesses plan to raise tech spend in 2026. Top needs: web development 39%, mobile apps 38%, software 37%, AI 32% (Clutch, Dec 2025).
  • E Most Clutch-reviewed projects fall between $10,000 and $49,999 (Clutch pricing, Sept 2026).
  • E Clutch rates: India $25–49/hr, US $50–99/hr. Accelerance 2026: senior Asia $31–41/hr, LatAm $60–75/hr.
  • P A custom MVP usually costs $15K–75K; offshore $10K–50K.

Trust and time zones

  • E Each extra hour of time-zone gap cuts live communication by about 11% (Organization Science, 2024).
  • P US leads say the India team is unreachable from about 2–6pm their time. LatAm firms sell workday overlap as their advantage.
  • P Indian firms that win US work publish exact overlap hours, write short direct updates, and sell business results rather than technologies.
  • E Deloitte 2024: cost is now the top reason for outsourcing for only 34%. 42% buy for specialised talent.

What AI changed

  • E Upwork Q2 2026: total spend down about 4%; AI-related work up 22%+ to about $330M.
  • E Upwork 2026 skills: AI integration +178%, AI chatbot development +71%. Full-stack is still the top coding skill.
  • E In 45% of tasks, AI-written code introduced security flaws (Veracode, 2025).
  • P Cleaning up apps built quickly with AI tools is a growing paid niche, at roughly $1.5K–8K.

Channels that firms like yours use

  • P E2M Solutions (India and California, about 200 staff) grew through agency coaches, event sponsorships and a fixed monthly white-label plan.
  • P Seahawk Media started from hosting-company partnerships and WordCamp.
  • P A white-label dev-shop owner says cold email to agencies has “collapsed”. He builds relationships with three people per agency instead.
  • P Well-researched trigger emails reportedly get 15–25% replies. No controlled study backs this.

Platform facts

  • E Clutch: the profile is free; you add client references and Clutch emails the clients. Ad packages need a 12-month commitment.
  • P Upwork: Agency Basic can't bid. Agency Plus is about $19.99 a month, and Connects cost $0.15 each. Specialized Profiles were removed in May 2026. Wins come from replying within an hour, in 2–3 sentences.
  • E Contra accepts studios and agencies, with no commission.
  • E Hacker News hiring threads ban agencies. r/startups allows promotion only in its pinned thread.
Rules that apply to every send. CAN-SPAM has no B2B exception. Every email needs an honest header and subject, must identify itself as an ad, must carry a valid postal address (your Houston private mailbox qualifies if it is registered with a commercial mail receiving agency), and must honour opt-outs within 10 business days. Penalties reach $53,088 per email. Most sales calls to business lines are outside the Do Not Call rules, but a small-business owner's mobile may be treated as residential. Never use autodialers or cold texts.

07 · Phase 6

The new messages

Plain text, under 90 words, no link or attachment in the first email, one true trigger, one question. Every email ends with the Houston signature, the postal address and the opt-out line from the company profile. Replace the braces with facts you have checked.

Before and after: the old day-5 email, rewritten for an agency

What changed, and why

  • The buyer changes from a trade owner to an agency owner, who already pays for development.
  • The opening uses a trigger the reader can check, their own hiring post, instead of an observation about a missing website.
  • The “we build websites” pitch is cut, since pitching lowers replies by up to 57% (Gong).
  • The demo link is removed: no links in the first email, for deliverability and trust.
  • The overlap hours are stated, which answers the main objection to an India-based team before it is asked.
  • “White-label, NDA, you own the code” covers the three things agencies worry about.
  • The ask changes from a 15-minute walkthrough to a small paid test, which is easier to say yes to.
  • The false deadline (“I'll take it down”) is gone. It doesn't fit a partner relationship.

09 · Phase 8

The 30-day test plan

No cold email is sent until the new domain is warm and scores 9/10 or better on mail-tester. LinkedIn, Upwork and the trust fixes fill the first two weeks.

Week 1
Oct 5–11
Fix and buildPause Gmail sends. Domain, inboxes, DNS records, start warm-up. Fix website claims, add the team page. Clutch profile and five reference requests. Build a list of 100 US agencies (directories, LinkedIn) and 50 open developer roles. Connect with 100 agency people on LinkedIn without a note.
Week 2
Oct 12–18
Warm channelsComment on prospects' posts. Message accepted connections. Upwork Agency Plus: 10 proposals on small jobs, each within an hour of posting. Publish two case-study pages. Run mail-tester and a seed test.
Week 3
Oct 19–25
Start email15 a day per inbox across 2 inboxes, about 150 for the week. Half to agencies (version A/B), half to developer roles (version A/B). Follow-ups as scheduled.
Week 4
Oct 26–Nov 1
Scale or switchRaise to 20–25 a day per inbox if bounces stay under 2% and nobody has complained. Run the weekly review and apply the decision rules below.

Decision rules

  • mail-tester under 9/10, or the seed test shows spam: no sends until it is fixed.
  • Bounces over 2% or any spam complaint: stop and re-verify the list.
  • After 300 delivered emails in a segment, positive replies under 1%: change the segment or the offer, not the wording.
  • Positive replies of 1–3%: test the subject line and opening line.
  • Over 3%: add a third inbox and scale.
  • Replies but no meetings: the offer is too big; switch to the smaller test.
  • LinkedIn acceptance under 20%: fix the sender's profile first.
  • No Upwork interviews after 20 proposals: narrow the job filter and reply faster.

What to expect in month one

About 400–500 cold emails, at the published average of one meeting per 344 emails, gives 1–2 meetings from email alone. LinkedIn and Upwork should add 2–4 more. A realistic month-one target is 4–6 discovery calls and 1–2 paid test projects. The month-one goal is to find which segment replies, not to make revenue.
